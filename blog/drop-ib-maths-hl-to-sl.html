IB Maths · HL or SL

Should I Drop from IB Maths AA HL to SL?

By Alex Ćetojević 13 September 2026 9 min read
← Back to Blog
In short

Drop to SL if you are heading for a 3 or 4 at HL, your target universities do not ask for HL maths, and the extra hours are hurting your other subjects. Stay at HL if a course you want lists Maths AA HL as a requirement, or if you are at a 5 and the problem is method and exam technique rather than understanding. Those problems are fixable in a term. Before you decide, ask your IB coordinator for the school's deadline. Most schools want the decision in the first term of Year 2.

First, the deadline

The IB lets schools change a student's level, but your school sets the date. Most schools want the decision made in the first term of the second year, often by October or November. After that the school may say no, or there may be a fee. Ask your IB coordinator for the exact date now, before you read the rest of this. The decision is much easier when you know how much time you have.

What actually changes when you drop

Both levels are graded 1 to 7 and both count the same towards your 45 points. So a 6 at SL and a 6 at HL are worth the same points. What changes is what universities see, and how much time you spend on maths.

  • Content. HL has everything in SL plus more. The extra topics are mostly in calculus, proof, complex numbers and vectors. If you drop, you stop those topics and keep the rest.
  • Exams. SL has two papers. HL has three. Paper 3 at HL is the long problem solving paper that most students find hardest.
  • Hours. HL is 240 teaching hours, SL is 150. In practice HL maths takes more homework time than any other IB subject.
  • Your other HL. You need three HL subjects. If maths drops to SL, another subject has to go up to HL, unless you already have four HLs.

Reasons to stay at HL

A course you want requires it. This is the one reason that overrides everything else. Many maths, physics, engineering, computer science and economics courses in the UK ask for Maths AA HL, often with a 6 or 7. LSE Economics, Imperial and Cambridge are well known examples. Some Dutch programmes ask for it too. Check the entry requirements page of every course on your list. Do not guess.

You are at a 5 and losing marks on method. A 5 at HL is usually not a sign that the maths is too hard. It is usually a sign of dropped method marks, weak exam technique, or one or two topics that were never properly learned. All of that can be fixed in a term with the right work. Students in this position who drop often regret it, because the same habits cost them marks at SL too.

You want to study something quantitative, even if it is not required. Economics, finance, data science and engineering all use HL level maths in the first year. A student who arrives with only SL will be catching up in the first term. HL is not just a ticket in. It is preparation.

Reasons to drop to SL

You are at a 3 or 4 and it is not moving. If you have put in real work since the start of Year 1 and you are still at a 4, that is honest information. A 4 at HL in your final results can cost you an offer. A 6 at SL is a stronger application for almost every course that does not require HL.

Your target courses do not need HL maths. Law, medicine, most humanities, most business courses in Italy and Europe, and US universities do not require Maths HL. Bocconi uses its own admission test and does not require HL. If none of your courses ask for it, HL is a choice, not a necessity.

Maths is dragging your other subjects down. If the hours you spend on HL maths are the reason your other grades are falling, the total points matter more. A 7, 6, 6 across three subjects beats a 5, 5, 5.

Your teacher and coordinator both recommend it. They have seen many students in your position. If they both say drop, take that seriously.

The alternative nobody mentions: switching course, not level

Some students at AA HL would do better at AI HL. It is still HL, so it still counts for universities that ask for HL maths in general. But be careful. Some courses ask for AA HL specifically, not AI HL. Maths, physics and engineering courses in the UK usually do. AI HL is not easier. It is different. It has more statistics and modelling and less proof and pure algebra. If you like the calculus but hate the proof, AI HL may fit. Read our AA vs AI guide before you consider it.

A four week test before you decide

If you are unsure, do not decide from feeling. Run a test. For four weeks:

  • Do every homework question with full working, as if it were the exam.
  • Each week, sit one past paper section under time. Mark it with the mark scheme, honestly.
  • Keep a list of every lost mark and why. Method, arithmetic, notation, or did not know how.
  • Fix the top two reasons on the list. Nothing else.

If your timed scores go up over the four weeks, stay. The problem was habits, and habits change. If they stay flat despite real work, drop, and do it without guilt. You now have evidence.

Questions to answer before you talk to your coordinator

  • Which of my target courses require Maths AA HL? Which say "preferred"?
  • What is my honest grade right now, on timed past paper questions, not homework?
  • Which topics are actually the problem? Is it two topics or all of them?
  • What is the school deadline?
  • If I drop, which subject becomes my third HL, and can I get a 6 or 7 in it?

Bring those answers to the meeting. The conversation will be short and useful.

A note for parents

Dropping a level is not a failure and it is not a shortcut. It is a resource decision. The question is where the hours will earn the most points and the best offers. Ask your child the questions above. If the honest answer is "I am at a 5 and I have not been doing the working", the fix is work, not a level change. If the answer is "I am at a 4 and I have been doing the work", the fix is probably the level change.

If you want an outside view, a free 20 minute call with one or two recent papers is usually enough to tell whether the gap is understanding or technique.

Frequently asked questions

When is the deadline to drop from Maths HL to SL?

Your school sets it. Most schools want the decision in the first term of Year 2, often by October or November. After that a change may be refused or may cost a fee. Ask your IB coordinator for the exact date.

Is a 6 at SL better than a 4 at HL?

For points, yes. A 6 is worth 6 points and a 4 is worth 4 at either level. For applications, a 6 at SL is better for any course that does not require HL maths. For a course that requires HL, a 4 at HL usually does not meet the offer either, so neither helps.

Which universities require Maths AA HL?

Mostly UK courses in maths, physics, engineering, computer science and economics, and some Dutch programmes. LSE Economics, Imperial and Cambridge quantitative courses are common examples. US universities and most Italian and European business courses do not require it. Bocconi does not. Always check the course page.

Do I need three HL subjects if I drop maths?

Yes. The IB requires at least three HL subjects. If maths goes to SL, another subject must be at HL, unless you already take four HL subjects.

Is AI HL easier than AA HL?

No. It is different. AI HL has more statistics, modelling and calculator work. AA HL has more proof, algebra and pure calculus. Some courses require AA HL specifically, so check before switching.

Can I go back up to HL after dropping?

Usually not in practice. The HL class will have covered topics you missed. Some schools allow it early in Year 2, but treat the drop as final when you decide.

What if I am at a 5 at HL?

A 5 is usually a technique problem, not an understanding problem. Dropped method marks, poor time management and one or two weak topics account for most of it. Run the four week test in this article before you decide.

Not sure if the gap is understanding or technique? Send one recent paper. A free 20 minute call is enough to tell.
