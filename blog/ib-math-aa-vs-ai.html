IB Math AA vs AI: Which Is Harder, and Which to Choose?

Written by Alex Ćetojević · Updated 16 November 2026

7 June 2026 · 11 min read

← Back to Blog
In short

For most students AA is the harder course. AA Paper 1 has no calculator, so the algebra, exact values and calculus are all done by hand. AI allows a calculator on every paper and centres on statistics and modelling. But choose by your degree, not by difficulty. Only a few courses ask for AA HL outright, such as Cambridge Engineering and TU Delft. Many others accept either course, and Bocconi and Politecnico di Milano do not mind which one you take.

IB Diploma students take one of two maths courses: Analysis and Approaches (AA) or Applications and Interpretation (AI). Both come at Standard Level and Higher Level, so there are really four options: AA SL, AA HL, AI SL and AI HL. The course decides what kind of maths you do. The level decides how deep you go. This page puts the two courses side by side, shows where each one gets hard, and lists what real universities ask for, with links to their own pages.

When a family asks me “AA or AI?”, the first thing I ask the student is: are you excited about solving an exercise by hand? That one question says a lot about which course will suit them.

The exams have almost the same shape. The real differences are the calculator rule and the kind of question. The details come from the IB’s subject guides for the two maths courses. The guides give Paper 3 one hour, but the IB exam schedules for November 2025, May 2026 and November 2026 give it 1 hour 15 minutes in both courses.

  • At SL, AI spends more time on statistics and functions (36 and 31 hours, against 27 and 21 in AA) and much less on calculus (19 hours against 28).
  • At HL, AA is built around calculus and geometry (55 and 51 hours). AI HL puts the most time into statistics (52 hours).
  • The totals are the same: 150 hours at SL and 240 at HL in both courses, with 30 of them for the toolkit and the IA.

Only in AI: at SL, Voronoi diagrams, loans and annuities, and the chi-squared test. HL adds matrices, graph theory, Markov chains, the Poisson distribution and the t-test.

Only in AA: proof, from simple proofs at SL to proof by induction and contradiction at HL, and Maclaurin series at HL. AA SL calculus also goes much further. It has the chain, product and quotient rules, while AI SL only differentiates and integrates simple powers of x. The full AA list is in the AA syllabus guide.

Which is harder?

For most students, AA. The main reason is Paper 1. With no calculator, every step is yours: factorising, exact trigonometric values, differentiating, simplifying. One slip early in a question can cost you the rest of it. AI students have a calculator on every paper, so the machine does much of that work.

The difference is easiest to see with the same maths asked both ways.

An AA style question. Find the exact maximum value of f(x) = xe−x. You differentiate with the product rule: f′(x) = e−x(1 − x). This is zero when x = 1, so the maximum is f(1) = 1/e. No calculator, and the answer must be exact.

An AI style question. A drug’s concentration in the blood, in mg per litre, is C(t) = 20te−0.5t, where t is the time in hours after the dose. When is the concentration highest, and what does that mean? You find the maximum on the calculator’s graph: about 14.7 mg per litre at t = 2. Then you say what it means: the drug is strongest two hours after it is taken.

The calculus underneath is the same. AA tests whether you can do it by hand, exactly. AI tests whether you can use a tool to find the answer and explain it in context.

That does not make AI easy. Its questions are long and full of context. Marks go to choosing the right model or test and to explaining what a number means.

In my lessons, the hardest part for AA students is finding a method to actually solve a calculation, where an AI student would use the calculator. For AI students it is the other way round. The conceptual questions are harder, because they are used to running methods on their calculator. When an exercise needs a different method, they are at a disadvantage.

Is AI HL harder than AA SL?

They are hard in different ways. AI HL has 240 teaching hours and a third paper, so there is much more to learn than in AA SL, which has 150 hours. AA SL has the paper with no calculator and more algebra by hand. Both are graded 1 to 7. But if a university asks for HL maths, AA SL does not meet it, however hard it feels.

So AA HL keeps every door open, and AI HL closes only a few, mostly in engineering. Two big Milan universities do not look at the course at all. Bocconi ranks you on its own test and your school grades. Politecnico admits on its own entrance test.

Even so, for Bocconi, Politecnico and Italian universities in general, I think AA is the more suitable course. The Bocconi test, for example, is taken without a calculator, so the work by hand that AA trains is exactly what it asks for.

Can you switch?

Usually, if your school allows it. Your IB coordinator decides. The first weeks of Year 1 are the easiest time. After the first term the gap gets hard to close.

The direction matters. Moving from AA to AI is easier, because the algebra you learned in AA still helps. Moving from AI to AA means catching up on algebra and calculus by hand, which is hard to do quickly.

I have not had a student switch course yet. But I have met students who wished they had picked AI instead of AA.

If the real question is the level, not the course, read whether to drop from Maths AA HL to SL.

The courses change from 2027

If you start the Diploma in August 2027 or later, you will be on new versions of both courses, first examined in May 2029. The current courses have their last exams in November 2028. Both AA and AI continue, so the choice between them stays the same. The AA syllabus guide explains which course you are on.

How to decide

  1. List two or three degrees you might apply to. Read the IB line on each course page. If one asks for AA HL, take AA HL.
  2. If none names a course, think about the kind of maths you want. Exact working by hand points to AA. Real data and a calculator point to AI.
  3. Then choose the level. HL if a course asks for HL maths, or if maths is one of your strengths. SL if not.
  4. Decide early. Talk to your coordinator in the first weeks of Year 1, while a switch is still easy.

I chose AA HL myself because I loved the conceptual side of mathematics. I got a 7.

Frequently asked questions

Is IB Math AA or AI harder?

For most students AA is harder, mainly because AA Paper 1 has no calculator and the course needs a lot of algebra by hand. AI is not easy, though. Its questions are long and set in context, and marks go to explaining results, not only finding them.

Is AI HL harder than AA SL?

They are hard in different ways. AI HL has 240 teaching hours and three papers, against 150 hours and two papers in AA SL. AA SL has a paper with no calculator. If a university asks for HL maths, only an HL course meets it.

Which IB maths do I need for engineering?

Check each course page. Cambridge Engineering and TU Delft ask for AA HL. Imperial Mechanical Engineering and ETH Zurich accept AI HL too. Politecnico di Milano admits on its own entrance test and does not look at the IB course.

Does Bocconi require IB Math AA or AI?

No. Bocconi sets no subject requirement and names both AA and AI, at SL or HL, as fine. It ranks you on its own test, or the SAT, and on your school grades.

Can I switch from AA to AI?

Usually, if your school allows it. The first weeks of Year 1 are the easiest time. AA to AI is easier than AI to AA, because the algebra from AA still helps in AI.

Is the IA the same in AA and AI?

Yes. Both are marked out of 20 on the same five criteria and count for 20% of the grade. Only Criterion E, Use of mathematics, is different between SL and HL.
