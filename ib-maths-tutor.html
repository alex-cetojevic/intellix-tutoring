01

Which course are you in?

Maths AA HL
The course universities ask for in maths, engineering, physics and a lot of economics. HL adds Paper 3 and a much bigger calculus section: proof by induction, complex numbers, series, differential equations. Students usually stall where the algebra gets long and there's no calculator to check it.
Maths AA SL
Same style as HL, less content, and still a Paper 1 with no calculator. Most of the marks I see lost here are algebra and notation slips. The 5 biggest are in AA SL common mistakes.
Maths AI HL
Statistics, modelling and technology, with a calculator on every paper. HL adds Paper 3 and topics like matrices, graph theory and statistical tests. Check your university course first: some accept AI HL, some ask for AA HL by name.
Maths AI SL
Calculator throughout and lots of real-world context. The maths is lighter and the reading is heavier. Marks go on turning a paragraph into the right model, and on using the GDC well.
Still choosing?

02

Taught the way the papers are marked

Method marks
IB mark schemes split most answers into M marks for the method and A marks for the answer, and an A mark usually depends on the M mark before it. So a right answer with no working can score less than you'd expect. The front of every paper says it: full marks are not necessarily awarded for a correct answer with no working.
Follow-through
One early slip doesn't have to sink a whole question. Examiners award follow-through marks when your later working is correct for the wrong value you carried forward. Students who show each step collect those marks. Students who jump to the end lose them.
Exact values and 3 s.f.
Unless the question says otherwise, answers go exact or to 3 significant figures. On AA Paper 1 that means leaving √3/2 as √3/2. Rounding too early in a long calculation is one of the cheapest ways to drop the final A mark.
Your personal traps
The derivative of sin(2x) is 2cos(2x). Writing cos(2x) is the slip I see most on Paper 1, and it costs marks on a question the student actually understood. We find your own list of these early, then drill them until they stop happening.

03

The Maths IA

What it’s worth
The exploration is 20% of your final grade at SL and HL, marked out of 20 across five criteria. Use of Mathematics carries 6 of those marks, and your topic decides how many of them you can reach. Full breakdown in how the 20 IA marks are split.
Where it goes wrong
The topic, almost every time. A topic that never needs more than substituting into a formula caps Criterion E before you've written a word. The other problem is timing: many schools want a first draft in the autumn of DP2, on top of everything else.
What support looks like
Testing topic ideas before you commit to one (start with how to pick a good AA topic), checking the mathematics is correct and pitched at your course, and reading drafts against each criterion (see what to fix before your first draft goes in). The exploration stays yours from start to finish: I give feedback, and I never write or edit any part of it.
When to start
At the topic stage. Two or three sessions there save far more marks than the same hours spent rescuing a finished draft.

Should I take AA or AI?
+
Start from the university course you're aiming at. Competitive engineering, maths and physics degrees generally ask for AA HL; many other courses accept either. The full comparison is in my AA vs AI guide, and you should always check your target university's own entry requirements.
Can you help with the Maths IA?
+
Yes. I help you test topic ideas, check the mathematics and read drafts against the five criteria. The exploration stays yours: I give feedback and never write or edit it. Starting at the topic stage makes the biggest difference.
What makes AA HL Paper 3 different?
+
It's one hour, calculator allowed, and built from two long problem-solving questions that walk you through an unfamiliar idea step by step. The maths inside is usually familiar. The hard part is keeping going when the setting isn't, so we practise past Paper 3s from early on.

Yes. Lessons run in English, Italian or Croatian. Your exam will most likely be in English, so we keep the maths vocabulary in English and switch language for explanations whenever that helps.
